L.A to O.C Plumbing Pro

A concept site for a family-owned plumber in Los Angeles and Orange County, built from the company's own Google reviews and job photos.

Trade
Plumbing
Area
Los Angeles & Orange County
Status
Concept, not commissioned
Made
October 2026
  1. The real logo, set as a stamped medallion instead of a generic icon.
  2. A catalogue cover in the logo's blue with gold lettering, so the company looks established before you read a word.
  3. Both phone numbers, Los Angeles and Orange County, one tap away on every screen.
  4. No made-up prices. The owner hadn't published any, so every service reads "by written estimate", the way he already works.

What went into it

Every review on the page is copied from the company's Google listing, word for word, with the customer's name and how long ago they wrote it. The job photos came from those same reviews.

The services list only what the company confirmed it does: drain cleaning and hydro-jetting, leak and shower valve repairs, water heaters and whole-home repiping. The quote form asks for the few things a plumber needs to call back.

It's hand-built in HTML, CSS and JavaScript, loads fast on a phone, and is kept out of search results so it never competes with the company's real listing.

002

Pink Salt Grill

A concept site for a family Filipino restaurant in Valencia, built from its own menu, its Google and Yelp reviews, and the look of its dining room.

Trade
Filipino restaurant and catering
Area
Valencia, Santa Clarita
Status
Concept, not commissioned
Made
November 2026
  1. The room, not a template. Cream walls, woven lamps and gold-leaf frames, all taken from the restaurant's own dining room.
  2. Open now, worked out live from the real hours, so nobody drives over on a Monday.
  3. Google and Yelp, side by side. 4.7 from 242 reviews and 4.3 from 450, each linking to the real reviews.
  4. Framed like their wall art. Every photo sits in a black shadow box with a thin gold line.

What went into it

Scroll down and the kitchen takes over as a short film in four cuts: the grill flares, sisig sizzles on a hot plate, pink salt falls on pork belly, and dinner lands on the table. Each cut dissolves into the next as you scroll, under a closing letterbox and a little film grain. The clips were AI-generated on a home PC with MiniMax H3, and the site says so.

Tablets and computers get separate pages for the menu, catering, reviews and hours, which helps search. On a phone the home page is one long scroll with everything on it.

All 37 dishes and prices come from the printed menu, 28 of them with real photos. Reviews are copied word for word from Google and Yelp. It stays out of search results so it never competes with the restaurant's real listing.
